Straight answers

What trades ask before they buy

Starting with the one everybody asks, which is whether it will embarrass you in front of a customer.

Is it going to talk rubbish to my customers?

Not unless you let it. Every message goes to you first — you read it on your phone and tap approve before it sends. Most people leave that on for a fortnight, get bored of approving the obvious ones, and switch it off for routine replies while keeping it on for anything over a value they set. It only quotes from your price book, and when a job is unusual it stops and hands it to you rather than guessing. It is your name on the van and the software is built on that basis.

I already use Tradify. Do I have to throw that out?

No. Tradify, Jobber, ServiceM8 and Commusoft are job management — they run the work after you have won it. Foreman is the bit before: answering, qualifying, quoting and chasing. It sits in front of what you already use and passes the won jobs through. If you are happy with your job system, keep it.

My quotes are too complicated for a computer to do

Some of them will be, and Foreman should not try. It handles the work you price the same way every time — a standard boiler swap, a split in a normal room, a cassette into an existing grid — which for most trades is the majority by volume and nearly all of the quotes that go out late. Anything outside that, it writes up the brief and hands it to you. The aim is to clear the routine so you have evenings back for the jobs that genuinely need your head.

How long before it is actually doing something?

About a week. You send your price list in whatever state it is in — a spreadsheet, a PDF, last year's quotes — and we build the price book with you. Then a divert on your number and a form on your site. There is no implementation project and no consultant.

I'm not technical

You do not need to be. On site the entire interface is a text message: Foreman texts you a summary, you text back yes or no. The dashboard exists for when you are sat down with a coffee, and plenty of customers barely open it.

What happens to my customer data and my prices?

They stay yours. Your customer records and your price book are never used to train any model, never shared with another customer, and never sold. If you leave we export the lot and delete our copy. Ask us for that in writing and you will get it — it should be in the contract, not just on a web page.

Does this replace my office manager?

No, and we would be wary of anyone who told you it did. It covers the hours they are not there — evenings, weekends, the heatwave week when the phone rings forty times — and it takes the chasing nobody enjoys off their desk. Firms that already have someone in the office usually find it makes that person better at the job, not redundant.

What if it does not work for me?

Stop at the end of the month. No contract, no minimum term, no exit fee, and we export your data for you. We would rather lose a customer cleanly than keep one who tells every other trade in the area that we tied them in.

Why is it £499 when other trade software is £50?

Because it does a different job. Job-management software is a filing cabinet — it is cheap because it waits for you to put work into it. Foreman is closer to hiring someone to answer the phone and chase quotes, which costs a great deal more than £499 a month. The honest test is whether it wins you one extra job you would otherwise have lost. On a £2,800 average ticket, one a month is roughly five times what it costs.

You are early access. What does that actually mean?

It means the software works but it is young, you will find things we have not, and we will fix them faster than we ever will again. In return founding customers pay half price for as long as they stay, and we build around how you actually work. If you would rather wait for something finished, we will tell you when it is — that is a perfectly sensible choice and we will not chase you about it.
